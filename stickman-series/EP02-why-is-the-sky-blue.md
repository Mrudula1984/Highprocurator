# EP02: "Why Is the Sky Blue… and Not Purple?"

**Format:** YouTube long-form, 16:9, ~80 sec · **Asker:** Sunny · **Explainer:** Blu (roles swapped from EP01)
**Hook twist:** Purple light actually bounces around the sky *more* than blue, so why don't we see a purple sky?
**Callback to EP01:** "Remember: sunlight is every color mixed together."

**Title options:**
- "Why Is the Sky Blue and NOT Purple? 🤯"
- "The Sky Should Be Purple. Here's Why It Isn't"
- "Why Is the Sky Blue? (Simple Answer in 60 Seconds)"

**Fact check (verified science):**
- Air molecules (nitrogen and oxygen) scatter short-wavelength light much more strongly than long-wavelength light (Rayleigh scattering ∝ 1/λ⁴). So blue and violet get bounced all over the sky.
- Violet scatters even more than blue, but (1) sunlight contains less violet than blue, (2) some violet is absorbed high in the atmosphere, and (3) our eyes are much more sensitive to blue than to violet. Result: we see blue.
- At sunset the light crosses far more air, so most of the blue gets scattered away before it reaches us, and the reds and oranges are left.
