# EP01: "Why Does a Rainbow Have Only 7 Colors?"

**Format:** YouTube long-form, 16:9, ~75 sec · **Asker:** Blu · **Explainer:** Sunny
**Hook twist:** *It doesn't.* The 7 colors are a human label, not physics.

**Title options:** "Rainbows Don't Have 7 Colors (Here's Who Decided They Do)" ·
"Why Does a Rainbow Have 7 Colors? The Answer Will Surprise You"

**Fact check (verified science):** Sunlight is a continuous spectrum. Raindrops refract, disperse, and reflect it,
so a rainbow is a smooth gradient with no borders. Isaac Newton named 7 colors (1670s–1704, *Opticks*).
He first described 5 and added orange and indigo, partly to match the 7 notes of the musical scale.
Human eyes have 3 types of cone cells, and our brain and language group the gradient into a few named colors.
